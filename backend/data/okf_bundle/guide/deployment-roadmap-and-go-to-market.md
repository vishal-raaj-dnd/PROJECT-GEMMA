## 18. Deployment Roadmap and Go-to-Market

| Phase | Timeline | Scope | Exit gate |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 0 Demonstrator | now | Simulated nodes, full workflow (Section 22) | Stakeholders can run it live |
| 1 Pilot | months 1-3 | 5-10 junctions, Operations Lane only, models calibrated, operator training | All minimum-acceptable thresholds met |
| 2 Zone | months 4-8 | 50-100 junctions, first RL agents (shadow then live), emergency-dispatch integration | Externality within threshold at zone scale |
| 3 City | months 9-18 | Full city coverage, multi-zone coordination, twin platform, police, fire and disaster integration; Identity Lane pilot only with legal sign-off | Independent audit of safety outcomes |
| 4 Evolve | ongoing | Regional expansion, V2X, predictive prevention, academic partnerships | n/a |

Each gate is an evidentiary checkpoint, not a schedule milestone: a phase does not begin until the previous phase's endpoints are read out.

**Go-to-market.** Pilot in one or two Tier-2 cities (population 20-50 lakh, over 3,000 accidents a year, an active Smart City board, commissioner-level sponsor). Convert pilot results into reference accounts through four channels: direct sale to Smart City corporations, tender response, system-integrator partnerships at about 70/30 revenue share, and emergency-services partnerships seeded with one free deployment. Then pursue 30-50 cities, defended by four moats: the accident dataset, cross-city model reuse, three- to five-year contracts with high switching cost, and the possibility of policy mandate.
