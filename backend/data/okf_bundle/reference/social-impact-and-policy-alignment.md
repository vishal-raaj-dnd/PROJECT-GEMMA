## 20. Social Impact and Policy Alignment

The lives-saved estimate is an ex ante model with stated elasticities against the 153,972 annual baseline. It assumes coverage of the full national camera estate, a strong assumption. At the year-five obtainable market of 25-35 cities, realised impact is roughly 5-8% of these values, and the two figures should never be quoted together without that distinction.

| Channel | Elasticity | Annual lives saved \[T\] |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Collision prevention | 5-8% of fatalities | 7,700-12,300 |
| Response-time improvement | 8-13% of fatalities | 12,300-20,000 |
| Infrastructure maintenance | 2-3% of fatalities | 3,100-4,600 |
| **Total** | **15-24%** | **23,100-36,900** |

At Rs. 1.08 crore per fatality this is roughly Rs. 25,000-40,000 crore a year. Response time dominates because it is where current practice is furthest from its achievable bound. Elasticities are drawn from trauma literature in other health systems and are unvalidated for Indian pre-hospital care.

**Policy alignment.** Contributes to SDG 3.6 (halving road traffic deaths and injuries) and the national target of a 50% fatality reduction by 2030, which witness-dependent detection cannot deliver. Traffic management is an explicit Smart Cities Mission priority with funds allocated and largely released, so the system is procurable within existing budget lines. Indigenous hardware and software with no foreign processing dependency align with domestic-technology preference in public procurement. Benefits by stakeholder:

- **Police:** tracking without high-speed pursuits, instant cloned-plate flags, court-ready evidence dossiers.
- **Municipal planners:** OD matrices without roadside surveys, hotspot diagnosis, modal-share data for bus lanes and EV charging.
- **Emergency services:** minutes saved and the right hospital on the first journey.
- **Citizens:** safer roads, faster repair of potholes and waterlogging, privacy by design.
